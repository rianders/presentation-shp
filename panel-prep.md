# Panel prep: map your project journey

For Rey Riordan (OSCE Simulation) and Jey Labadorf (Lexus), ahead of the panel.

The panel works best if the room can see where each project traveled, not just where it ended up. Fill in the six steps below for your project. Short answers are fine, a line or two each. Rick will use them to build a journey slide for each of you, and they double as the spine of your 10–15 minutes.

## The six steps

1. **How it started.** Did the faculty member find you, or did you find them? What was the project supposed to be at that point?
2. **Where it ran at the start.** Your laptop, Colab, a departmental machine, AWS, GCP, something a faculty member paid for out of pocket?
3. **What changed along the way.** How the project itself changed, and what changed in the AI tech between when you started and now.
4. **Who you had to navigate.** The units, offices, and people at Rutgers you had to work through, and what each one needed from you.
5. **Where it runs now.** The current hosting, and who pays for and maintains it.
6. **What you'd tell the next student.** What you wish you'd known at the beginning.

## What Rick is asking on stage

These are the discussion questions, so nothing will surprise you:

- Looking back, what would you have liked to know at the beginning?
- How did you host your project to get started?
- What changed in the AI tech from beginning to end?
- How is the current version hosted?
- What advice would you give faculty starting a new project?
- What support would have helped: administrative, IT, departmental?

## Format

- 10–15 minutes each to tell your experience
- 15 minutes of discussion
- Then audience questions

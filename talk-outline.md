# From Classroom to Campus Service

*Lessons from faculty who teach and research AI*

**Speaker:** Rick Anderson, Director of Emerging Technology, Rutgers UOES
**Audience:** SHP and IT faculty · **Length:** 45 minutes
**Program:** After Tom Vossler (AI IT Working Group), before Andy Paige and Eric Marshall (Cloud Services and OARC)

**How to use this outline:** Each section is one slide. The slide holds a headline and at most one line. Rick tells the story, so the details live in the telling, not here. Story slots list candidates only.

**The pathway:** Teaching the tech → Research with and about the tech (the two overlap) → Student and departmental use → University services.

**The pain point:** Faculty don't have AI-familiar support. Different kinds of infrastructure fit teaching, research, or both, and integrating with the central service tech Rutgers offers is complicated and undocumented for this kind of use.

## 3 · The pain point (6–10 min)

- **On screen:** "Faculty don't have AI-familiar support."
- **Say:** Different kinds of infrastructure fit teaching, research, or both. Integrating with central service tech is complicated, and it isn't documented for this kind of use.
- **Story:** Laura Ramírez, the security document for her recent research. A live example of the support we need to address. The entire project runs on a Mac laptop, with local-only AI through Ollama. It involves anonymized PHI data, which is why it needs the security document. Research without PHI doesn't require the same document unless it's being moved to supported infrastructure for research, commercialization, or hosting of some kind.

## 5 · Stage 1: Teaching the tech (13–19 min)

- **On screen:** Headline only.
- **Tools (say, don't show):** Colab, Hugging Face, OpenRouter
- **Story:** Jim Samuel. His students get a real client and use the latest AI tech to support them. They evaluated how accurately LLMs carry emotion in translation, then moved to guardrails for a postpartum chatbot.

## 6 · Stage 2: Research with and about the tech (19–26 min)

- **On screen:** Headline only.
- **Tools (say, don't show):** Amarel, Chameleon Cloud, local AI (Ollama)
- **Say:** Faculty research can include local AI like Ollama. To scale, it has to move off the work laptop to other platforms or secure places to run it.
- **Stories:** Sonia Yaco, "What can AI do with special collections?" (The American Archivist, 2025), co-authored with Bala Desinghu (at OARC then, Harvard's Kempner Institute now), Claire Warwick, and me; and Chameleon, automated with AI agents to make IT management easier
- **Bridge to Stage 3:** LLEXIS started as a research project too, before the Dean's grant
- **Reference:** `research/chameleon.md`, `research/yaco-paper.md`

## 7 · Stage 3: Student and departmental use (26–33 min)

- **On screen:** "Who hosts it, secures it, and backs it up?"
- **Story:** LLEXIS. It got the Dean's grant and student support (Rahul Mittal and Jey Labadorf)

## 8 · Stage 4: University services (33–39 min)

- **On screen:** "Can homegrown edtech and research be supported?"
- **Tools (say, don't show):** Portkey, Shibboleth, RU Cloud (AWS)
- **Say:** These are centrally supported tools, hosted or managed by OIT and Andy. Especially in these emerging areas. The projects aren't stalled; they do what's necessary. Integrating with central service tech is complicated and isn't documented for this kind of use.
- **Story:** VHS. A chatbot/GPT project becoming a standalone service project for students.

## 9 · The questions for Rutgers (39–42 min)

- **On screen:**
  - Can we identify these projects early?
  - Can we carry them from research to hosting with students?
  - Can central services be documented for this kind of use?

## 10 · Closing thoughts (42–44 min)

- **On screen:** "Students are building this" and "The student perspective: Rey and Jey, on a panel later today"
- **Say:** Student work is a real situation. Without any dedicated resource, it's a practical decision to have students be part of building these solutions. They should be part of solving the issues and learning from it. The question that arises: can we create more support for them?
- **Point ahead:** Rey Riordan and Jey Labadorf join me on a panel later today, on their experience as student support on faculty projects that grew into student services.
- **What the panel covers:** the learning experience, the faculty support experience, University administration, the security hoops they had to jump through, and what third-party help they got.

## 11 · Up next (44–45 min)
